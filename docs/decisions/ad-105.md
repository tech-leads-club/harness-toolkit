# AD-105 — Anyone may use it, including a company; nobody may sell it as a service, and the notices travel with it

- **status**: active

## Decision

1. **Elastic License 2.0** replaces PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0. SPDX `Elastic-2.0`, declared in
   `package.json`, `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.
2. **Any use is free**, by a person or a company, commercially or not, at no cost and with no separate licence to
   negotiate.
3. **Two prohibitions**: providing the software to third parties as a hosted or managed service that gives them a
   substantial set of its features, and removing or obscuring the copyright and licence notices.
4. **Attribution is a licence condition.** `NOTICE` travels with every distributed copy.

## Why

**The old licence forbade the use this exists for.** PolyForm Noncommercial permits use "for any purpose other
than a commercial purpose", and a company running the harness on its own repositories, as part of its own work, is
a commercial purpose. So the audience — teams inside companies — was the audience the licence excluded, and every
adoption needed a separate negotiation with the author.

**The thing to prevent was never commercial use. It was somebody selling this work.** Elastic License 2.0 draws
that line where the author wanted it: use it, change it, ship it inside your product, run it across a company —
but do not turn it into a service you charge others for, and do not strip the name off it.

**Attribution needed to be enforceable rather than requested.** Under ELv2 the notices are a condition of the
grant: remove them and the licence is not being complied with. That is stronger than a request in a README and it
is the author's stated priority.

**A non-compete licence was considered and rejected.** PolyForm Shield and the Functional Source License both
protect a business the licensor is running. There is no such business here, so their central clause would have
bought nothing while costing the same adoption friction. `Elastic-2.0` also has no change date, which matches the
intent: the terms are not a countdown to something else.

## Trade-offs

**It is source-available, not open source.** No OSI approval, so corporate OSPO policies, distribution
repositories and scanners such as FOSSA, Snyk and `license-checker` refuse it by default. That is the price of the
two prohibitions and it is stated in the README rather than discovered.

**"Hosted or managed service" is the licence's line, not a revenue test.** A consultancy charging for work that
uses the harness is not selling the harness; a platform that offers the harness to its customers as a feature is.
The distinction lives in the licence text, and nothing here restates it in other words.

**The change is one-way in practice.** Relicensing was possible because the copyright is held by one person — 249
of the human commits are the author's and the rest are the release bot. A second contributor makes the next change
a negotiation, which is an argument for settling this now rather than later.

**Published versions keep the licence they shipped under.** Every tarball on the registry carries the `LICENSE`
that was in it, so 0.4.6 and earlier remain PolyForm Noncommercial for whoever installed them. The new terms bind
from the next release forward.

## Not decided here

- **A contributor licence agreement.** Nothing accepts outside contributions yet; when it does, the question of who
  may relicense comes back and it should be answered then.
- **A commercial exception.** Nobody is being charged, so there is no second licence to write. If that changes, the
  hosted-service prohibition is exactly the hook a paid grant would hang from.
- **Dual licensing under an OSI licence.** It would remove the scanner friction and also remove both prohibitions,
  which is the whole content of this decision.
