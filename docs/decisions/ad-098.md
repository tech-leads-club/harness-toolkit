# AD-098 — One copy of the core, not twenty-four

- **status**: active

## Decision

1. **One `bun build` invocation with every entrypoint and `--splitting`.** A chunk can only be shared between
   entries the bundler sees together.
2. **Entry names stay flat and keep `.mjs`.** `--entry-naming "[name].mjs"`, because the launcher resolves
   `dist/<entry>.mjs` and every installed machine's hooks reach it through the launcher.
3. **Chunks live in `dist/chunks/`**, named with a content hash, and the directory is deleted before each build.
4. **Three rails on the built output**: every source entrypoint has a flat bundle, every relative import an entry
   makes resolves on disk, and no chunk is orphaned.

## Why

**Measured, before: 5.0 MB of `dist/` for 548 KB of distinct code.** 24 bundles at 257–280 KB each. Two of them —
`stop.mjs` and `tool-before.mjs` — share 149 KB of byte-identical lines, so more than half of every bundle is the
same core copied again. The npm tarball was 1.6 MB packed and 5.9 MB unpacked, and the runtime home carried
another copy.

**After: 548 KB, 24 flat entries of 1.9–22.6 KB plus 13 chunks.** The entry the launcher runs went from 262,090
bytes to 4,677.

**It also costs less per hook, by less than it first appeared.** Timed 40 runs of `tool-before` interleaved
between the two layouts to avoid measuring warm-up: 41 ms against 37 ms. A first, non-interleaved pass showed
48 against 36, which did not survive interleaving — the honest number is around 4 ms, roughly 10%, and the
reason is that Node parses 4.7 KB plus the chunks the entry actually reaches instead of the whole inlined core.

**The value is mostly the disk and the download, not the speed.** 9× on the package, on every install, and on
every `update` that re-materialises the runtime.

**Nothing about the hooks changes.** They name `bin/tlc-exec.mjs <entry>`, the launcher resolves
`dist/<entry>.mjs`, and both keep their names. That is why the entry naming is pinned rather than left to the
bundler's default, which would have written `dist/src/entrypoints/stop.js`.

## Trade-offs

**A hook now opens more files than one.** Fewer bytes, more `open` calls. The measurement above is the net of
both, and it favours splitting on this machine; a filesystem where `open` is expensive relative to parsing could
land the other way.

**A chunk that goes missing is a dead hook, and nothing else here would notice** — the suite imports source, not
`dist/`. Hence the rails: every entry's relative imports are checked against disk, and the orphan check catches a
chunk left behind by a stale build.

**Hashed chunk names accumulate.** The directory is replaced on every build for that reason. A build interrupted
between the delete and the write leaves no chunks at all, which fails loudly rather than shipping a stale one.

**`dist/` is not committed** ([/decisions/ad-097.md](/decisions/ad-097.md)), so nothing compares bytes between two
machines' builds. Bun remains the only bundler for the same reason it was before: two bundlers produce two
artefacts.

## Not decided here

- **Whether the entries could be smaller still.** 22.6 KB for `stop.mjs` is the code that is genuinely its own.
- **Shipping `src/` alongside `dist/`.** The Bun path resolves from source, so both are in the tarball; the trade
  is unexamined ([/decisions/ad-012.md](/decisions/ad-012.md)).
- **Whether Node's own module cache would make a resident process cheaper.** Hooks are one process per event by
  the host's design, so there is nothing to keep warm.
